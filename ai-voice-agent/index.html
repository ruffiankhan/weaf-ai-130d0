AI voice agents and AI receptionists for Australian businesses

By Adnan Khan, AI automation consultant, Point Cook, Melbourne. Last updated 17 October 2026.

An AI voice agent, also called an AI receptionist, answers your business phone in a natural voice, 24 hours a day. It answers common questions, takes the caller's details, books appointments into your calendar and hands anything complex or urgent to you, then sends you a summary of every call. WEAF AI, run by Melbourne AI consultant Adnan Khan, sets these up for Australian businesses and has already set up AI voice calling automations for businesses in Sydney.

Key takeaways

  • An AI voice agent suits businesses that miss calls because they're on the tools, with a client or closed.
  • A good setup answers only from information you've approved and passes anything it can't handle to a person.
  • Published Australian AI receptionist prices mostly sit between about $99 and $699 a month, plus setup. See the AI receptionist cost guide.
  • Before launch, check your privacy notice, how callers are told about recording, and where recordings are stored.

What does an AI voice agent actually do?

It does the parts of reception that follow clear rules, at any hour, without putting callers on hold. A typical setup:

  • Answers common questions in your business's words: hours, service areas, what you do and don't do, and price ranges you've approved
  • Takes the caller's name, number, suburb and what they need
  • Qualifies the enquiry, such as job type, urgency or whether they're a new or existing client
  • Books, moves or cancels appointments in your calendar or booking system
  • Texts or calls back people who hung up or called while you were busy
  • Sends you a summary and transcript of every call
  • Transfers the call, or texts you straight away, when a person is needed

What it shouldn't do: give legal, medical or financial advice, quote firm prices for custom work, or promise anything you haven't approved. Those calls go to you.

How does a call with an AI receptionist work?

  1. A customer rings your number. The AI either answers every call, or your phone forwards to it when you don't pick up, when the line is busy or after hours.
  2. The agent greets the caller with your business name, says it's an AI assistant and mentions recording if calls are recorded.
  3. It works out what the caller wants and answers from the information you've approved.
  4. It collects the details you need: name, number, suburb, the job or appointment type and how urgent it is.
  5. It books a time in your calendar, or takes a message if booking doesn't suit that call.
  6. If the call is urgent or the caller asks for a person, it transfers the call or sends you an immediate text.
  7. The caller gets a confirmation text. You get a summary and transcript, logged in your CRM if you use one.

What affects the cost of an AI voice agent?

  • Call volume and length. Many plans include a set number of calls or minutes and charge for extra.
  • Per call or per minute. Long calls cost more on per-minute plans.
  • Integrations. Booking into your calendar, CRM or practice software takes more setup than taking messages.
  • How many call types it handles. One after-hours script is simpler than a full receptionist.
  • Self-serve or managed. Managed services usually charge a setup fee and a higher monthly fee.

Vendor guides checked in October 2026 put Australian AI receptionist services between about $99 a month for self-serve tools and $1,299 or more for enterprise plans. What should Australian businesses check for privacy and call recording?

This is general information, not legal advice. If you handle health or financial information, get advice for your situation.

Privacy Act

The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner says "the Privacy Act applies to all uses of AI involving personal information". Its guidance asks businesses to update privacy policies and notices to cover AI use, to clearly label public-facing AI tools, and to do due diligence on the product rather than "set and forget". Collect only what you need. Health information is sensitive information, so clinics need extra care.

Call recording consent

There's no single nationwide rule for recording calls in Australia, and state and territory laws differ. The practical approach, as Sprintlaw's guide to business call recording recommends, is to tell callers clearly that the call will be recorded before recording starts. An AI voice agent makes this easy because it says the same opening line on every call.

Where the data is stored

Ask any provider where recordings, transcripts and summaries are held, who can access them, how long they're kept and how deletion works. Many voice platforms run on overseas infrastructure, and sending personal information overseas brings extra obligations under Australian Privacy Principle 8.

Follow-up texts

If follow-up messages promote your business, the Spam Act applies. The ACMA's spam rules require consent, clear identification of your business and a working unsubscribe option.

Frequently asked questions

Will callers know they're talking to an AI?

They should. A well-built agent says it's an AI assistant in its greeting, and the OAIC recommends clearly labelling public-facing AI tools. Most callers care more about getting a quick answer or a booking than about who picks up.

Can I keep my existing business number?

Usually, yes. Most setups forward your existing number to the AI when you don't answer, when the line is busy or after hours. Some providers give you a new number instead, so check which option a provider uses before you sign up.

Can an AI voice agent book appointments into my calendar?

Yes, if your calendar or booking software can connect to it, either directly or through a connector tool such as Zapier, Make or n8n. If it can't, the agent can take the booking request and text it to you to confirm.

Can an AI voice agent speak languages other than English?

Many voice platforms support several languages. Adnan Khan has built a bilingual AI voice agent himself. Test with real speakers of each language before relying on it.

How much does an AI receptionist cost in Australia?

Published Australian prices mostly range from about $99 to $699 a month, plus a setup fee from $0 to around $1,000, according to vendor guides checked in October 2026. Overage charges, phone numbers, integrations and GST can add to that.

Do I need to be technical to use one?

No. You need to know what your callers ask and how you want calls handled. The technical setup can be done by a provider or a consultant, or by you with a self-serve tool.
