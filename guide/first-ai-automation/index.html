How to start your first AI automation

A five-step guide for small business owners. By Adnan Khan, AI automation consultant, Melbourne. Updated 17 October 2026.

Most businesses don't need an "AI strategy" to get started. They need one automation that works, saves real hours and gives them the confidence to do the next one. This guide walks you through picking that first automation and launching it safely, without hiring a developer.

The short version: pick one repetitive task that follows clear rules, map it as trigger, steps and output, use the simplest tool that can do it, run it next to the manual process for two weeks, and measure the hours saved.

Step 1: List the repetitive work

For one normal week, keep a running note of every task you or your team repeat more than twice. Write down roughly how many minutes each one takes. Don't filter yet. Common entries look like this:

  • Replying to new enquiries and sending the same information each time
  • Chasing people who missed a call or didn't confirm a booking
  • Copying details from emails or forms into a CRM or spreadsheet
  • Writing up quotes, invoices or meeting notes
  • Sending reminders and follow-ups

Step 2: Score each task and pick one

Give each task a score from 1 to 5 on three things, then multiply them.

FactorAsk yourself
FrequencyHow often does it happen each week?
TimeHow long does it take each time?
Cost of a slipWhat does it cost when it's late or missed, such as a lost lead or an unpaid invoice?

Start with the highest-scoring task that follows clear rules. "When a form comes in, add it to the CRM and send the price list" is a great first automation. "Decide which clients to fire" is not.

Step 3: Map the steps

Write the task as three parts:

  1. Trigger: what starts it, such as a missed call, a new form entry or an email with an attachment.
  2. Steps: what a person does today, in order. Mark any step where someone has to read or write text and use judgement.
  3. Output: what "done" looks like, such as a text sent, a CRM record created or a draft reply ready for approval.

If you can't write it down, it isn't ready to automate. Fix the process first.

Step 4: Choose the simplest tool that works

Work through these in order and stop at the first one that can do the job:

  1. Features you already pay for. Your booking system, CRM or accounting software often has reminders, templates and rules built in.
  2. A connector tool such as Zapier, Make or n8n, which moves information between apps when something happens.
  3. An AI model such as ChatGPT or Claude, only for the steps that read or write text, like summarising an email or drafting a reply.

Keep a person approving anything that goes to a customer until you trust the output.

Mind the data

Before you connect customer information to an AI tool, check where that tool stores data and whether it uses your data for training. Australian businesses covered by the Privacy Act need to handle personal information carefully, and that includes the tools you send it through. When in doubt, keep sensitive details out.

Step 5: Run it next to the manual process, then measure

For two weeks, let the automation run while the manual process carries on as normal. Check every output. Then compare:

  • Hours saved per week
  • Errors or awkward outputs, and why they happened
  • Leads or payments that would have slipped through before

If it's reliable, switch the manual process off and move to the next task on your list. One working automation a month adds up fast.
