How much does an AI receptionist cost in Australia?

2026 prices, hidden costs and a worked example. By Adnan Khan, AI automation consultant, Melbourne. Last updated 17 October 2026.

An AI receptionist in Australia typically costs between $99 and $699 a month, plus a one-off setup fee of $0 to about $1,000. Self-serve tools start at about $99 to $149 a month, managed services run about $200 to $500 a month with $400 to $1,000 setup, and premium or enterprise plans run from $500 to $1,299 a month or more. For comparison, a part-time human receptionist costs about $35,000 a year once on-costs are added.

Short answer: budget roughly $1,200 to $8,000 a year for an AI receptionist, depending on call volume and how much it does. Before you sign, check per-minute or per-call overage, phone number fees, setup, integrations and whether GST is included. Figures come from Australian vendor guides checked in October 2026 and are indicative only.

What hidden costs should you check?

  • Overage. Basic Solutions says overage is often $1.25 to $2.50 a minute once included minutes run out. Its example: a $149 plan with 300 minutes, plus 200 extra minutes at $2, comes to $549 for the month.
  • Per call or per minute. Per-call pricing (TalkMate's $0.45 an extra call) suits long calls; per-minute pricing (Kaylo's $0.80 a minute) suits short ones.
  • Phone numbers. A dedicated number can add a monthly fee, and your carrier may charge for forwarded calls.
  • Setup. From $0 to $1,000 or more. Ask exactly what it covers.
  • Integrations and SMS. Calendar, CRM and text messages can be extra.
  • Introductory pricing. Deskie's $99 rises to $149 after 12 months, for example.
  • GST. Some prices exclude GST or add tax at checkout. Valory's guide recommends confirming currency and GST, then pricing the whole service including cancellation terms.

To estimate minutes, multiply your monthly calls by the average call length. For example, 150 calls at 3 minutes each is 450 minutes. On a plan with 300 included minutes and $2 overage, that's another $300 a month.

Will an AI receptionist pay for itself?

Work it out with your own numbers. Here's an illustrative example only, with assumptions stated, for a trade business that misses calls while on the tools.

AssumptionValue
Missed calls a month30
Share that would have become a booked job20% (6 jobs)
Average gross profit per job$250
Profit currently at risk each month6 × $250 = $1,500
AI receptionist cost$300 a month + $600 setup spread over 12 months = $350 a month
Break-even$350 ÷ $250 = 1.4 extra jobs a month

In this example the service pays for itself if it wins about two extra jobs a month. Be conservative: some missed callers ring back anyway, so count profit rather than revenue and track real bookings for the first two months. If you miss only a handful of calls, a missed-call text-back may be enough, and it costs far less.

Frequently asked questions

Is an AI receptionist cheaper than a human receptionist?

Usually, yes. Vendor guides put AI receptionists at roughly $1,200 to $8,000 a year, against about $35,000 a year for a part-time receptionist with on-costs. A human still handles complex, sensitive or relationship calls better.

Do AI receptionist prices include GST?

Not always. Some Australian providers publish prices excluding GST, and some add tax at checkout. Confirm the currency and GST before you compare plans.

Do I pay per call or per minute?

It depends on the provider. Some plans include a set number of calls and charge per extra call; others include minutes and charge per extra minute. Estimate your monthly calls and average call length to see which suits you.

What's the cheapest way to stop missing calls?

A missed-call text-back that sends a booking link, or voicemail-to-text, often costs far less than a full AI receptionist and may be enough if you miss only a few calls a week.
